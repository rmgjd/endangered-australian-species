Australia's threatened animals

The national list tells us which animals face extinction risk. Monitoring tells us how some populations are changing. Together, they reveal where the challenge is greatest—and where our evidence is still thin.

The first figure comes from the Australian Government's EPBC list extracted 28 August 2026. The monitoring figures come from the 2025 Threatened Species Index (TSX), whose data run through 2022. A listed entry may be a species or a subspecies. These measures have different coverage and should not be compared as if they describe the same sample.

01 / THE LIST

Risk reaches every animal group

The EPBC list is a snapshot of nationally recognised risk. Its categories describe extinction risk, not the number of animals alive.

02 / THE MAP

Geography changes the story

Listed animals occur across state borders. The monitored bird samples declined in every jurisdiction, while national priority places identify where conservation action is focused.

03 / THE TREND

Monitoring adds a harder measure

The TSX brings repeated surveys together into an index of average relative abundance. It does not estimate how many animals are left.

04 / THE RESPONSE

Recovery needs evidence as well as action

Australia's plan targets 110 priority species and 20 priority places. Monitoring coverage remains incomplete, so activity and outcomes must be described separately.

THREE SPECIES · THREE KINDS OF CHALLENGE

Risk has no single cause

These examples show why a legal status alone cannot describe the work needed for recovery.

FISH · ENDANGERED

Maugean skate

The only confirmed population lives in Macquarie Harbour, Tasmania. Low dissolved oxygen and degraded water quality are central challenges. Government action includes harbour oxygenation, captive management and monitoring.

Species profile

BIRD · CRITICALLY ENDANGERED

Orange-bellied parrot

This migratory bird needs habitat in both Tasmania and the mainland. The Action Plan identifies winter saltmarsh loss, small population size, altered fire and disease; actions include habitat work, nest boxes and captive breeding.

Species profile

MAMMAL · CRITICALLY ENDANGERED

Northern hairy-nosed wombat

A very small, restricted population faces habitat degradation, predators and genetic vulnerability. Priority work includes protected habitat, predator exclusion and establishing additional populations.

Species profile

What the evidence supports

National listing shows breadth of extinction risk across animal groups. The monitored TSX samples show major long-term declines, especially among amphibians and reptiles, while also exposing gaps in coverage. Priority places and species show where governments are directing effort. Whether those actions reverse declines needs continued, comparable monitoring.
