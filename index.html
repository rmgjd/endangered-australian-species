The work of recovery

Action needs evidence that lasts.

Australia's plan targets 110 priority species and 20 priority places. Knowing where effort goes is one step; detecting recovery requires continued monitoring.

Why movement changes recovery

Protection must connect the seasons.

Summer breeding habitat is only one part of the annual cycle. Mainland saltmarsh habitat and migration stopovers also matter.

The official profile identifies King Island along the migration route. These connections explain why recovery involves habitat work across jurisdictions.

Three species, three conservation challenges

Recovery has to fit the animal.

Fish · Endangered

Maugean skate

The only confirmed population lives in Macquarie Harbour, Tasmania. Low dissolved oxygen and degraded water quality are central challenges.

Response: harbour oxygenation, captive management and monitoring.

Read the species profile

Bird · Critically Endangered

Orange-bellied parrot

This migratory bird needs habitat in Tasmania and on the mainland. Winter saltmarsh loss, small population size, altered fire and disease complicate recovery.

Response: habitat work, nest boxes and captive breeding.

Read the species profile

Mammal · Critically Endangered

Northern hairy-nosed wombat

A small, restricted population faces habitat degradation, predators and genetic vulnerability.

Response: protected habitat, predator exclusion and additional populations.

Read the species profile

What comes next

Keep watching. Keep protecting.

The list establishes the breadth of recognised risk. Monitoring reveals serious declines within the observed samples. Priority species and places direct conservation effort. Continued, comparable surveys are needed to show whether that effort changes the trajectory.
